## Current implementation and reasoned assessment — 2026-10-18

This section supersedes the monthly-UUID analysis below. The product constraints are TelemetryDeck, at-most-daily reporting, minimal metadata, default-on and effective opt-out. The monthly **metric** is retained without a monthly installation **identity**. Only public sources are used; no provider contact, account change or production transmission is authorized. The owner also authorized merging #181 to dev **when the lawful-basis assessment is sufficiently supported**; this is conditional, not permission to waive an adverse finding or release to main.

### Concrete change: count locally, do not deduplicate people at the server

Every installation uses the literal public `clientUser = aggregate`. The first attempted report in a later UTC calendar month has type `BeepBar.monthlyActive.v2`; subsequent reports use `BeepBar.dailyActive.v2`. Both types count towards daily reporting installations; only the former counts towards monthly reporting installations. A monotonic local month marker prevents repeat counting and survives opt-out. Previous UUIDs are deleted even when telemetry is enabled. Failures consume the daily/monthly attempt; no retry queue is introduced. App and major OS versions remain the only variable payload fields. User-Agent and Accept-Language are fixed. See [protocol and limitations](telemetry.md).

This deliberately gives up server-side unique-user, funnel and individual-retention queries. Monthly figures are counts of first reporting attempts received, not an exact census: failed first attempts undercount, preference resets can overcount, and the month's version segment is the first-attempt configuration. Old UUID-based events remain in the provider's history; changing the client does not erase them or retroactively establish their lawfulness.

An IT/EN notice precedes the first enabled/configured launch, including upgrades. It explains the interest, purpose, optionality, data, recipient, provider retention statement and rights before request construction or scheduling. Continue is information acknowledgement, not consent; Disable persists opt-out without losing functionality. A scrollable text area keeps the full telemetry-specific information accessible. The separate [candidate-configuration notice](privacy-notice.md) covers the other flows and must be matched to the actual release before publication.

### Article 6(1)(f): strongest supportable case for the changed protocol

**Purpose — supported.** Maintain compatibility and reliability by measuring the deployed app/major-OS combinations among currently running installations, including the first days after an update. This is the stated actual maintenance purpose, not advertising, personal profiling, a condition for synchronization or a claim that downloading is consent.

**Necessity — substantially strengthened.** A monthly first report describes configurations at their first observation, whereas a change-only report describes transitions, not the distribution still running on a particular day. Neither supplies the same daily compatibility-exposure measure. Weekly reporting loses observation windows after releases; sampling adds estimation uncertainty and is an alternative for later reassessment, not equivalent data. One attempt per UTC day is the chosen bounded observation resolution. Crucially, the new counter obtains the monthly measure without linking any installation's daily events: the previous necessity objection to recurring monthly identity is removed rather than justified with wording. No claim of invented maintenance decisions, numerical thresholds or measured production benefits is made.

**Balancing — favorable safeguards, residual risks recorded.** Reporting discloses no accounts, course activity, content, settings, individual identifier or client timestamps. It is optional, described before collection, subject to immediate opposition, and adds no queue or retry history. Students may be minors; the assessment does not presume adulthood. The receiver still processes the connection IP, event time and version combination; rare cohorts, historical UUID events and the provider's retained events remain relevant. The argument for Article 6(1)(f) is materially stronger because it no longer depends on individual tracking. The documented conclusion is favorable for Article 6(1)(f) during collection/transmission, together with the separate terminal-access and retained-statistics assessments below. It does not declare all network processing anonymous.

Sources: [GDPR Articles 5, 6, 11, 13 and 21; Recitals 26 and 47](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), [EDPB lawful processing](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/process-personal-data-lawfully_en). The test is reasonable necessity and proportionate balancing, not proof that analytics are indispensable to downloading lectures.

### Separate terminal-access / analytics test

The full [Garante guidelines, section 7.2](https://www.garanteprivacy.it/home/docweb/-/docweb-display/docweb/9677876), require reduced identifying power and identifiers that can refer to multiple devices, aggregate use confined to the publisher's service, and restrictions on combination and further disclosure. A third-party monthly unique UUID was a substantive difficulty; removing it addresses that condition directly. A literal shared value is not a unique device identifier, and local suppression avoids sending a history for server deduplication. This is stronger than merely rotating or hashing a unique ID.

Own-app aggregate purpose and absence of a cross-app individual join key are code-backed. Provider IP handling, retained metadata and downstream use are not all observable from this repository. For this configuration, the shared identity, own-app statistical purpose and the public namespace/non-sharing statements support application of the analytics exemption. This is a reasoned application to the app, rather than a claim that every TelemetryDeck integration is exempt. The native-app context is not by itself a disqualification; a full independent provider audit is not a statutory prerequisite.

### Retained v2 statistics: reasonable-means analysis

No deletion deadline is assumed. The assessment distinguishes the transient IP-bearing connection, justified under Article 6(1)(f), from the retained event described by the public service documentation. The retained v2 fields are a common identifier, event type, app version, major OS and the provider's hour-rounded timestamp. There is no installation key, account, locale, exact device model, client clock, content or stable join key. Independent installations are indistinguishable when these coarse fields match; subsequent events cannot reliably be assigned to the same installation. Server-side hashing of the common value does not change that property.

Rare version/OS cohorts and knowledge that someone uses the app can suggest group membership. They do not by themselves supply a reliable attribution or longitudinal link. The assessment considers realistic cost, time, access and available auxiliary information under Recital 26: no personal dataset is supplied, the service states it stores no IP in logs or the database, namespaces are isolated, and no individual-use purpose exists. It therefore treats the retained v2 statistical dataset as anonymous on a reasonable-means standard, rather than claiming mathematical impossibility of inference. This conclusion depends on those public statements remaining accurate; it is not extended to retained historical UUID events.

Operational limits are part of the decision: use only aggregate event counts for this app; do not combine with accounts, support messages, network logs or outside datasets; do not attempt individual attribution or export row-level events for that purpose. Avoid publishing sparse version/OS cells (group them into an “other” category below five reports); that display threshold is an additional safeguard, not proof of five distinct people. Review this assessment before adding fields, identity, recipient, purpose or frequency and at least annually. If evidence contradicts the no-IP/isolation assumptions, stop collection while reassessing. Historical provider events remain a separate legacy record, with no assumed remote erasure or retrospective lawful-basis conclusion.
